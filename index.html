Automatic Proof of Work

A single rugged badge, worn by each worker, that records who worked, where they went, and what work they did. No phones, forms, apps, equipment tags, GPS, or cellular. Actual is in development: the architecture and design are done, and we're looking for engineers to build the first prototype.

The Problem

The way time and tool use are tracked in the field today takes more effort than it should and still leaves gaps. Paper timesheets depend on workers remembering to fill them out. Phone apps assume coverage, battery, and willingness. Vehicle GPS can show where a truck went but not what the worker actually did.

The result: payroll cleans up mistakes, supervisors chase down missing hours, and managers don't get a clear view until the week is over. Every downstream decision — billing, job costing, scheduling, estimating — starts on shaky ground.

What We Want to Change

Crews — Wear the badge. Skip the timesheet. Hours get recorded without anyone filling anything out.

Supervisors — Review exceptions instead of reconciling every timecard.

Managers — See where crews are and how jobs flow — this week, not last month.

Owners — Bid from real production data. Pay from verified hours. Trust the numbers.

Ops — See how the day splits between driving, power equipment, hand labor, and idle time.

The Truth of Work

Every record tells a story. The goal is to make it the right one.

The badge is designed to capture what truly happens on a job as crews move — time, movement, and the physical signature of the work itself. Data would be stored on the badge and offloaded when the worker returns to the shop, creating a timestamped activity stream that is free of edits or recollection bias.

What it's designed to produce:

  • Verified start/stop per worker and crew
  • The driving route and every job site visited, scheduled or not
  • The on-foot work path across each property
  • Active labor broken down by type: driving, idle, power equipment, hand labor

This is the truth layer — the data a business could pay from, report from, and lead from. When work is logged as it happens, every downstream decision starts on solid ground.

Reverse-Engineering

You can't improve what you can't see flow.

Once work is captured truthfully, the next question is why it flowed the way it did. The same data should make it possible to reverse-engineer how jobs really run — where time goes, where constraints live, and where assumptions fall short.

a. Reverse-Engineered Schedule

  • Planned vs. actual task durations per property and route
  • Setup, travel, and delay patterns across the day
  • Hidden bottlenecks and repeatable flow patterns

b. Reverse-Engineered Estimate

  • Convert badge data into cost per task and property
  • Ground estimating models in observed work, not guesses
  • Continuously refine future bids and plans from real production rates

Truth becomes improvement. The data that powers payroll would also teach how to plan better — no extra steps, no separate system.

How It's Designed to Work

Edge Capture — The badge is designed to work fully offline. Inertial sensors, barometric terrain profiling, and kinetic and acoustic edge AI log movement and labor signatures all shift. No coverage needed, nothing to install at job sites.

Cloud Reconstruction — Back at the shop, the badge offloads its log. Because every shift starts and ends at a known point, the cloud can use bi-directional inertial smoothing and road-plus-elevation map matching to correct drift, reconstruct the route, find each job site, and map work on each property. Results are written to an immutable store, and anomalies are flagged for review.

Downstream — Verified data flows into the systems the business already uses: payroll, job costing, scheduling, billing. Every downstream record carries its origin — the operator, the timestamp, the badge attestation.

Cost Model (Design Targets)

These are the economics we're designing toward. They drive the hardware and cloud decisions: commodity-cost hardware, and cloud pricing that scales with the size of the operation.

The hardware would be bought once — one badge per worker, designed to be cheap and replaceable, with no tags, beacons, or data plans. A recurring cloud fee would track the scale of the operation: the sites serviced or the crews run. 50 badges in summer, 15 in winter? The fee adjusts. No unused licenses. No per-worker math.

Hardware — One-time purchase, owned by the customer. Target: about $30 per badge.

Cloud — Monthly fee based on the size of the operation, covering data processing, storage, and the truth store.

Connectors — Per active integration per month. Pay only for what you connect.

The Promise

These are the design commitments we're building to. The badge is not a tracker — it is a witness. It will not record conversations. It will not monitor behavior. It captures the basic facts of the job: when you arrived, where you moved, what kind of work you did, when the day wrapped up. It only records what the work creates.

The person doing the work should never have to prove they worked. Actual puts the truth where the work happens. You wear the badge. It records what really happened.

Simple always wins. One badge. One worker. One automatic record of the truth.
