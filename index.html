Automatic Proof of Work

A single rugged badge, worn by each worker, that records who worked, where they went, and what work they did. No phones, forms, apps, equipment tags, GPS, or cellular. Actual is in development: the architecture and design are done, and we're looking for engineers to build the first prototype.

The Problem

Tracking time and tool use in the field takes too much effort and still leaves gaps. Paper timesheets depend on workers remembering to fill them out. Phone apps assume coverage, battery, and willingness. Vehicle GPS can show where a truck went but not what the worker did.

So payroll cleans up mistakes, supervisors chase down missing hours, and managers don't get a clear view until the week is over. Billing, job costing, scheduling, and estimating all start on shaky ground.

What We Want to Change

Crews: Wear the badge. Skip the timesheet. Hours get recorded without anyone filling anything out.

Supervisors: Review exceptions instead of reconciling every timecard.

Managers: See where crews went and how jobs flowed this week, while it still matters.

Owners: Bid from real production data. Pay from verified hours. Trust the numbers.

Ops: See how the day splits between driving, power equipment, hand labor, and idle time.

The Truth of Work

Every record tells a story. The goal is to make it the right one.

The badge is designed to record time, movement, and the physical signature of the work as crews move through the day. Data stays on the badge until the worker gets back to the shop, then uploads as a timestamped activity stream that nobody edits and nobody has to remember.

What it's designed to produce:

  • Verified start/stop per worker and crew
  • The driving route and every job site visited, scheduled or not
  • The on-foot work path across each property
  • Active labor broken down by type: driving, idle, power equipment, hand labor

This is the truth layer: data a business could pay from, report from, and lead from.

Reverse-Engineering

You can't improve what you can't see flow.

Once work is captured, the next question is why it flowed the way it did. The same data could show how jobs really run: where time goes, where the constraints are, and where estimates fall short.

a. Reverse-Engineered Schedule

  • Planned vs. actual task durations per property and route
  • Setup, travel, and delay patterns across the day
  • Hidden bottlenecks and repeatable flow patterns

b. Reverse-Engineered Estimate

  • Convert badge data into cost per task and property
  • Base estimates on observed work
  • Refine bids and plans from real production rates

The data that runs payroll would also improve planning, with no extra steps and no separate system.

How It's Designed to Work

Edge capture: The badge works fully offline. Inertial sensors, a barometer, and on-device models for motion and sound log movement and labor all shift. No coverage needed, nothing to install at job sites.

Cloud reconstruction: Back at the shop, the badge uploads its log. Every shift starts and ends at a known point, so the cloud can correct sensor drift, match the day against road and elevation maps, rebuild the route, find each job site, and map the work on each property. Results go to an immutable store, and anomalies are flagged for review.

Downstream: Verified data flows into the systems the business already uses: payroll, job costing, scheduling, billing. Every record carries its origin: the operator, the timestamp, and the badge attestation.

Cost Model (Design Targets)

These are the economics we're designing toward. They drive the hardware and cloud decisions: commodity-cost hardware, and cloud pricing that scales with the size of the operation.

Customers would buy the hardware once: one badge per worker, cheap and replaceable, with no tags, beacons, or data plans. A recurring cloud fee would track the sites serviced or the crews run. If you run 50 badges in summer and 15 in winter, the fee follows. No unused licenses or per-worker math.

Hardware: One-time purchase, owned by the customer. Target: about $30 per badge.

Cloud: Monthly fee based on the size of the operation, covering data processing, storage, and the truth store.

Connectors: Per active integration per month. Pay only for what you connect.

Who It's For

Outdoor crews that move, build, and maintain. Landscaping, construction, utilities, municipalities, solar, facilities.

Millions of U.S. field workers rely on timesheets, phones, or truck GPS. Each worker is a potential badge.

They need hours and tool use recorded truthfully without extra steps, so payroll is correct, supervisors aren't chasing paperwork, and owners can trust job costing.

The Promise

These are the design commitments we're building to. The badge is a witness. It will not record conversations or monitor behavior. It captures the basic facts of the job: when you arrived, where you moved, what kind of work you did, when the day wrapped up. It only records what the work creates.

The person doing the work should never have to prove they worked. You wear the badge. It records what really happened.
